# Stela Homepage Content Blueprint

Status: Approved v1.0  
Source language: English  
Scope: Homepage content only. This document does not prescribe layout, imagery, typography, or interaction design.

## 1. Homepage Job

The homepage must help a non-technical visitor understand Stela without prior context.

After five seconds, the visitor should understand:

- Stela permanently identifies physical assets.
- The first applications are vehicles and solar panels.
- The identifier is placed directly on the asset, not only on a removable label.
- The physical identifier is connected to a digital record that can be checked later.

After thirty seconds, the visitor should understand:

- why labels, serial plates, and paperwork are not always enough;
- how the mark, recorded event, and certificate work together;
- how this may help with theft deterrence, recovery, insurance, ownership, and lifecycle records;
- that Stela is in development and technical validation, not yet a commercially available product.

The desired visitor summary is:

> Stela permanently identifies cars and solar panels and creates a digital record, so the real asset can still be verified later.

## 2. Strategic Position

### Public category

Permanent asset identification.

### Core promise

An identity that stays connected to the asset.

### Concrete product definition

Stela is developing a portable system that places a unique identifier directly on an approved asset surface and links that identifier to a verifiable digital record.

### Initial market focus

1. Vehicles and insurance.
2. Solar panels and distributed energy assets.

### Long-term direction

Stela can become an identity layer for physical assets across insurance, ownership, maintenance, transfer, financing, audit, and recovery workflows.

The long-term direction should follow the concrete product story. It must not replace it.

## 3. Audience Priority

The homepage must be understandable to everyone, but it should convert institutional visitors in this order:

1. Insurers and owners of valuable asset portfolios.
2. Vehicle, solar, and asset-industry pilot partners.
3. Manufacturers, installers, technical validation partners, and laboratories.
4. Strategic and financial investors.
5. Interested members of the public.

The primary homepage conversion is a pilot or use-case conversation. Investor conversion remains available through the navigation and investor page, but should not dominate the homepage.

## 4. Brand and Naming

- **Stela** is the sole public-facing company and system name.
- **SafeSingleMark S.L.** is the legal company name and should appear only where legally or formally relevant.
- **Verid** is retired and must not appear in public copy, code-facing labels, metadata, or future translations.
- **Decision Confidence Infrastructure** remains an internal strategic framework. Its ideas may inform the copy, but the term should not appear on the homepage.
- The physical device should be described functionally as the **Stela marking system** until a product name is formally selected.
- The digital output may be called the **Stela record** or **Stela certificate**, depending on context.

## 5. Message Hierarchy

Every section must reinforce this sequence:

1. The real object needs a lasting identity.
2. Removable identifiers can break the connection between an asset and its records.
3. Stela places an identifier directly on the asset.
4. Stela records the authorized marking event.
5. The identifier and record can be checked later.
6. This may improve deterrence, recovery, insurance, and lifecycle management.

Do not begin with trust, infrastructure, certificates, cryptography, or the marking machine. Begin with the asset and the problem of keeping its identity attached.

### Section 2: The Problem

**Purpose:** Explain the failure in ordinary language before introducing the system.

**Eyebrow**

The identity problem

**Headline**

A record is only useful if it still points to the right asset.

**Body copy**

Labels can be removed. Serial plates can be replaced or hidden. Paperwork and databases can become separated from the object they describe.

When that happens, an owner, insurer, buyer, or authority may have information about an asset without a reliable way to show that the information belongs to this specific physical object.

Direct marking methods such as acid etching, sandblasting, and laser marking address part of this problem by placing an identifier on the asset itself. But a physical mark does not automatically preserve who created it, how the process was controlled, or what evidence supports the resulting identity.

**Closing statement**

Stela is designed to keep the asset and its identity connected.

### Section 3: How Stela Works

**Purpose:** Explain the complete system in three plain-language steps.

**Eyebrow**

How it works

**Headline**

Mark the asset. Record the event. Verify it later.

**Step 1: Mark**

A trained operator uses the portable Stela marking system to place a unique identifier directly on an approved part of the asset.

**Step 2: Record**

The authorized session records which asset was marked, who performed the work, which machine was used, when and where it happened, and what supporting photographs were captured.

**Step 3: Verify**

The identifier is linked to a Stela record and certificate that permitted users can check when the asset is insured, transferred, inspected, recovered, or retired.

**Supporting note**

The marking process, approved surfaces, and evidence workflow remain subject to technical and regulatory validation.

### Section 4: Why It Matters

**Purpose:** Translate the system into outcomes that matter outside the technology team.

**Eyebrow**

Value over time

**Headline**

Useful long after the mark is made.

**Outcome 1: Make irregular resale harder**

A visible identifier attached to the asset is harder to remove or conceal without leaving evidence of interference.

**Outcome 2: Help identify recovered property**

An asset found away from its original owner or installation can be checked against its recorded identity.

**Outcome 3: Support insurance decisions**

Insurers can receive clearer evidence about which asset was marked, when the work occurred, and which authorized parties were involved.

**Outcome 4: Preserve lifecycle identity**

The same identity can remain relevant as the asset is maintained, transferred, insured, inspected, recovered, or eventually retired.

These outcomes are intended benefits to be tested through validation and pilot programs. They must not be presented as proven commercial results before evidence exists.

### Section 5: Initial Applications

**Purpose:** Make the first two market wedges concrete and distinct.

**Eyebrow**

Where Stela starts

**Headline**

Two industries where identity needs to stay with the asset.

**Application 1: Vehicles and insurance**

Stela is designed to place a controlled identifier on an approved area of vehicle glass and link it to the vehicle's recorded identity.

For owners and insurers, this could make the vehicle harder to disguise, help identify recovered property, and provide clearer evidence during insurance and ownership checks.

**Relevant parties**

Vehicle owners, insurers, dealerships, fleet operators, authorized marking partners, and recovery authorities.

**Application 2: Solar panels**

A panel's rear label can be removed, replaced, damaged, or hidden after installation. Stela is designed to place the identifier on a validated, non-active area of the panel's front glass so that it remains attached to the panel itself.

The Stela record can connect that physical identifier to the manufacturer's serial number, installation, location, owner, and marking evidence.

**Relevant parties**

Panel manufacturers, installers, plant operators, asset owners, insurers, financiers, maintenance providers, and authorities.

### Section 6: More Than Engraving

**Purpose:** Prevent Stela from being understood as only an engraving company.

**Eyebrow**

The complete system

**Headline**

The mark is the anchor. The record creates the lasting value.

**Body copy**

The physical mark connects the digital record to a specific real-world object. The recorded marking event explains how that connection was created. The certificate makes the relevant information available for later verification.

Together, these elements are designed to answer practical questions:

- Is this the asset that was originally recorded?
- Who marked it, and under whose authority?
- When and where did the marking happen?
- What evidence supports the record?
- Has the asset's status or ownership changed since then?

Stela is intended to complement insurer, manufacturer, registry, and asset-management systems already in use. It should add a stronger connection to the physical asset without requiring organizations to replace their existing software.

### Section 7: A Clear Comparison

**Purpose:** Explain the difference without technical jargon or unsupported competitor claims.

**Headline**

Not every permanent mark creates a reliable identity.

**Introductory copy**

Different identification methods solve different parts of the problem. Labels are simple and inexpensive. Acid etching and sandblasting are established field-marking methods. Industrial lasers can provide precise digital control. Stela is designed to combine controlled field marking with an authorized record and later verification.

**Labels and printed codes**

Easy to apply and useful for routine identification, but they can be removed, replaced, damaged, or hidden.

**Acid etching**

Creates a visible identifier directly on glass and can be applied in the field. It uses a chemical process, and the mark alone does not create an authorized digital record of the work.

**Sandblasting**

An established method for marking glass directly. It uses abrasive media, while process control, operator authorization, evidence capture, and certification normally remain separate concerns.

**Industrial laser marking**

Can provide precise and digitally controlled marking. Many systems are designed for factory or fixed-equipment environments, although field applicability varies by system and material.

**Stela**

A field-oriented diamond-marking process designed to operate without chemical etchants or abrasive blasting media, combined with an authorized record of the marking event and a certificate that can be checked later.

**Qualification**

The homepage must not present this as universal superiority over every competing method. Current differentiation should be described in terms of system design and workflow. Comparative claims about depth, durability, repeatability, speed, surface integrity, resistance to removal, or cost require controlled test evidence.

## 9. Public Claim Boundaries

### May be stated as current fact

- SafeSingleMark S.L. is developing Stela.
- The project is based in Vigo, Galicia.
- The intended first applications are vehicles and solar panels.
- The system combines an on-asset identifier with a digital record and certificate.
- The product is pre-commercial and undergoing development and validation.
- The founders bring vehicle-security and software-security experience.

### Must be qualified as design intent or potential value

- permanence and resistance to removal;
- compatibility with tempered, laminated, solar, architectural, or industrial surfaces;
- no effect on structural integrity, coatings, optical performance, or warranties;
- theft deterrence, higher recovery, fraud reduction, or insurance savings;
- legal, forensic, regulatory, or evidentiary value;
- integration with public registries, insurers, manufacturers, DMS, CMMS, or ERP systems;
- marking time, depth, weight, accuracy, repeatability, and operating cost.

### Do not publish without specific validation and source review

- market-size and theft-volume claims;
- financial projections or franchise economics;
- performance comparisons presented as established fact;
- claims that marks cannot be removed;
- claims that Stela has no equivalent competitor;
- named partnerships that have not been formally approved for publication;
- patent counts, granted protection, or defensibility claims before legal confirmation;
- claims that certificates are accepted by insurers, courts, authorities, or regulators.

### Competitive claim ladder

**May be stated during development**

- Stela is designed around a digitally controlled diamond-marking process.
- The intended process does not use chemical etchants or abrasive blasting media.
- The intended workflow combines marking, operator and machine authorization, evidence capture, a digital record, and later verification.
- Acid etching, sandblasting, labels, and laser marking have legitimate strengths and solve different parts of the identification problem.

**May be stated after controlled laboratory comparison**

- measured marking depth and repeatability;
- edge quality and legibility;
- effects on glass, coatings, optical performance, and mechanical integrity;
- resistance to abrasion, weathering, concealment, or removal;
- marking time, operator consistency, and process failure rates;
- performance relative to specified acid, sandblasting, or laser methods under equivalent test conditions.

**May be stated after operational pilots**

- effects on theft deterrence, irregular resale, and recovery;
- improvements to insurance, inspection, transfer, or maintenance workflows;
- reductions in claim uncertainty, handling time, fraud, or operating cost;
- user acceptance, training time, field reliability, and commercial value.

Comparative claims must identify the tested method and conditions. Avoid broad statements such as **better than acid**, **deeper than sandblasting**, or **more permanent than laser** without a defined comparison and supporting evidence.

## 10. Comprehension and Editorial Tests

Before implementation, test the English copy with people who have no prior knowledge of Stela.

### Five-second test

Ask:

1. What does Stela do?
2. What kinds of things is it for?
3. What is the main benefit?

The answer should mention permanent identification, vehicles or solar panels, and a record that can be verified later.

### Thirty-second test

Ask the visitor to explain the system in their own words. They should describe:

1. a mark placed directly on the asset;
2. information recorded when the mark is made;
3. later verification of the asset and record.

### Credibility test

Ask:

1. Does any statement sound exaggerated?
2. Is it clear that the system is still in development?
3. Is the difference between Stela and ordinary engraving clear?

### Editorial rules

- One main idea per section.
- One outcome per paragraph.
- Prefer ordinary verbs: mark, record, check, identify, recover, insure, transfer.
- Explain the concrete mechanism before the strategic vision.
- Do not repeat the same three-step explanation in multiple sections.
- Keep technical caveats close to the claim they qualify.
- Do not force a fixed number of headline lines across languages. Line breaks are a presentation decision, not part of the source meaning.
