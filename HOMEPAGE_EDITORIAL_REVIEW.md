# Stela Homepage Editorial Review

Status: Completed against approved semantic wireframe v1.0  
Reviewed source: `HOMEPAGE_SEMANTIC_WIREFRAME.md`  
Scope: Message comprehension, credibility, production length, and conversion logic

This is an internal review. It is not public website copy and does not replace testing with people who have no prior knowledge of Stela.

## Executive Finding

The homepage now has a coherent, literal story:

> Stela is developing a system that permanently identifies vehicles and solar panels by placing an identifier directly on the asset, recording the authorized marking event, and making that identity available for later checks.

The narrative no longer depends on visitors understanding abstract ideas such as trust infrastructure, provenance, or physical-digital systems. It begins with a familiar problem, explains a visible action, and ends with practical uses.

The English source copy is approved for design and translation. External comprehension testing remains recommended as a validation step before public launch.

## First-Time Visitor Stress Test

### Question: What does Stela do?

**Expected answer**

It puts a lasting identifier directly on a vehicle or solar panel and connects it to a digital record.

**Copy that supports the answer**

- Hero category and headline.
- Hero supporting sentence.
- Three-step explanation.

**Assessment**

Pass. The answer is available in the first screen without relying on a diagram.

### Question: Why is that useful?

**Expected answer**

Labels and records can become separated from an asset. Stela is intended to keep the real object connected to its identity so it can be checked later.

**Copy that supports the answer**

- Identity Problem section.
- Value Over Time section.
- Complete System section.

**Assessment**

Pass. The problem is stated in ordinary language before benefits are introduced.

### Question: Is Stela an engraving company?

**Expected answer**

No. The physical mark is one part of a controlled process that records the operator, machine, time, place, photographs, and later verification.

**Copy that supports the answer**

- How Stela Works.
- The Complete System.
- Identification Methods comparison.

**Assessment**

Pass. The three-step process prevents the marking action from becoming the whole category.

### Question: Is the system available now?

**Expected answer**

Not yet. It is pre-commercial and undergoing development and validation with the intention of running focused pilots.

**Copy that supports the answer**

- Hero status line.
- Development and Partnership section.

**Assessment**

Pass. Maturity is clear without making development the opening message.

## Institutional Visitor Stress Test

### Insurer or asset owner

The page explains the possible operational value: stronger asset matching, recovery checks, clearer marking evidence, and identity across ownership or lifecycle changes. The copy avoids promising reduced claims, lower premiums, or proven theft outcomes.

**Assessment:** Pass.

### Vehicle or solar pilot partner

The page identifies both initial applications, relevant physical surfaces, the intended evidence workflow, and the areas still requiring validation.

**Assessment:** Pass, subject to technical confirmation of the approved marking locations.

### Technical or laboratory partner

The page distinguishes current system design from claims that require testing. The development section identifies material safety and repeatability as validation priorities without turning the homepage into a specification sheet.

**Assessment:** Pass.

### Investor

The page communicates category, initial wedges, differentiated workflow, combined team experience, current maturity, and partner strategy. It does not overload the general visitor with market sizing, projections, or intellectual-property claims.

**Assessment:** Pass. Detailed investor evidence belongs on the investor page and in approved materials.

## Competitive Position Stress Test

The comparison makes two arguments in sequence:

1. Removable identifiers can lose their connection to the asset.
2. Direct marking alone does not necessarily control, document, and preserve the complete identity workflow.

This allows Stela to challenge both labels and direct marking alternatives without claiming that established methods are useless.

The most defensible current distinction is:

> Stela is being designed as a controlled identification system, not only as a method for making a mark.

The page must not currently claim that Stela is deeper, safer, faster, more durable, or more effective than acid etching, sandblasting, or laser marking. Those comparisons should be introduced only after controlled testing establishes the relevant conditions and results.

## Editorial Changes Made in v0.2

1. Changed the hero from a present-tense deployment claim to an active development statement.
2. Replaced **across decades of operation** with **through long service lives** to avoid implying validated lifespan.
3. Replaced **attached to the asset** with **placed directly on the asset** so the language does not resemble labels.
4. Reframed software compatibility as the intended role of Stela rather than a completed integration capability.
5. Removed intellectual-property strategy from the public development summary because it does not help a general visitor understand the product.

## Recorded Editorial Decisions

### 1. Public use of “permanent”

**Recommendation:** Keep it as the category and ambition. Pair it with explicit validation language until material testing supports precise durability claims.

### 2. Record versus certificate

**Recommendation:** Use **Stela record** for the continuing digital identity. Use **certificate** only for a specific issued or shareable representation of that record. Do not use the words interchangeably.

### 3. Who can verify

**Recommendation:** Retain **permitted users can check** until product policy determines what is public, permissioned, role-based, or owner-controlled.

### 4. Combined experience

**Decision:** Keep the section focused on the team's combined operational and digital expertise. Do not publish the founders' names on the website. Fact-check the collective experience claim and any supporting archival material before publication.

### 5. Primary conversion

**Recommendation:** Keep **Discuss a pilot** as the persistent action. It is specific enough to qualify serious visitors and honest about company maturity.

## External Comprehension Test

Test the production copy as an unstyled document before visual design. Recruit at least five readers who have not previously discussed Stela.

### Procedure

1. Show only the hero for five seconds.
2. Hide it and ask the three five-second questions.
3. Let the participant read through How Stela Works.
4. Ask them to explain the product without using wording from the page.
5. Let them read the complete page.
6. Ask what they believe is available today, what remains in development, and what they would do next.

### Five-second questions

1. What does the company do?
2. What kinds of assets is it for?
3. What is connected to what?

### Full-page questions

1. What problem does Stela solve?
2. How does it work?
3. How is it different from a label or ordinary engraving?
4. Who would benefit from it?
5. Is it already commercially available?
6. What, if anything, sounded exaggerated or unclear?

### Passing threshold

At least four of five participants should independently communicate all four ideas:

- the identifier is placed directly on the physical asset;
- vehicles and solar panels are the first applications;
- the marking event creates or supports a digital record;
- that record can be checked later.

No participant should leave believing that Stela is already commercially deployed, that the mark is impossible to remove, or that comparative performance has already been proven.
